EXPLANATION OF USE CASE DIAGRAM:
1. Primary Actors:
   - Guest / Public Visitor: Unauthenticated user who can browse the hospital donor directory, filter donors by ABO/Rh group and city, and initiate registration. Sensitive PII (phone/email) is shielded.
   - Registered Donor: Authenticated user with role `DONOR`. Can access their personal dashboard, toggle availability between Available and Unavailable, view their status change audit trail, and inspect verified past donations.
   - System Administrator: Authenticated staff member with role `ADMIN`. Governs clinical operations, records emergency blood requisitions, moves requests through their lifecycle (`PENDING` -> `IN_PROGRESS` -> `FULFILLED`), records completed blood collections, and activates/deactivates accounts.
2. Relationships & Includes:
   - "Record Blood Donation" (UC-13) unconditionally includes "Update Donor Last Date" (UC-17) because logging a donation automatically updates the donor's eligibility and last donation timestamp.

EXPLANATION OF CLASS DIAGRAM:
1. Architecture Layers:
   - Entity Layer: Represents persistent database tables mapped with JPA annotations (`@Entity`, `@Table`, `@Id`, `@ManyToOne`, `@OneToOne`).
   - Repository Layer: Spring Data JPA interfaces (`DonorRepository`, `BloodRequestRepository`, etc.) extending `JpaRepository` with custom `@EntityGraph` definitions to eliminate N+1 queries.
   - Service Layer: Business logic interfaces and implementations (`DonorServiceImpl`, `DonationHistoryServiceImpl`, etc.) governed by `@Transactional`.
   - Controller Layer: Spring MVC controllers handling HTTP requests, performing session verification, populating UI models, and dispatching to JSP views.
2. Core Relationships:
   - `User` to `Donor`: One-to-One (`1:1`). Every donor profile corresponds to exactly one login credential.
   - `BloodGroup` to `Donor`: One-to-Many (`1:N`). Many donors share one standardized blood group entity (A+, B+, etc.).
   - `Donor` to `DonationHistory`: One-to-Many (`1:N`). A single donor can have a rich chronological record of past donations.
   - `Donor` to `DonorAvailabilityHistory`: One-to-Many (`1:N`). Every availability toggle is immutably logged for audit purposes.
   - `BloodRequest` to `BloodGroup` and `User`: Many-to-One (`N:1`). Requests reference the demanded blood group and the administrator who created the request.

EXPLANATION OF ER DIAGRAM:
1. Tables & Keys:
   - `users`: Stores login identities for both Admins and Donors. Primary key is `user_id`. `email` has a unique index (`uq_users_email`).
   - `blood_groups`: Master lookup table with 8 rows (`A+`, `A-`, `B+`, `B-`, `AB+`, `AB-`, `O+`, `O-`). Primary key is `blood_group_id`.
   - `donors`: Core medical entity. Foreign key `user_id` is unique (`uq_donors_user_id`), enforcing a strict 1-to-1 relationship with `users`. Foreign key `blood_group_id` links to `blood_groups`. Composite index `idx_donors_search (blood_group_id, city, availability)` ensures millisecond search speeds.
   - `donation_history`: Child table of `donors`. Stores verified donation events. Foreign keys link to `donors.donor_id` and `users.user_id` (`recorded_by`).
   - `blood_requests`: Requisitions created by healthcare facilities and administrators. Foreign keys link to `blood_groups.blood_group_id` and `users.user_id` (`created_by`).
   - `donor_availability_history`: Append-only audit table capturing the before and after state whenever a donor flips their availability flag.
2. Cardinalities:
   - `users` (1) to `donors` (1): Exact One-to-One.
   - `blood_groups` (1) to `donors` (N): One-to-Many.
   - `blood_groups` (1) to `blood_requests` (N): One-to-Many.
   - `donors` (1) to `donation_history` (N): One-to-Many.
   - `users` (1) to `donation_history` (N): One-to-Many (Recorded By).
   - `donors` (1) to `donor_availability_history` (N): One-to-Many.
