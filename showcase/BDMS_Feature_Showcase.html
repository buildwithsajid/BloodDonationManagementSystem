1. Problem & Solution

The problem

  • In an emergency, families search social media and phone lists to find a matching blood donor.
  • There is no trusted, searchable list of donors who are available right now.
  • Public lists expose donor phone numbers and invite spam.
  • Hospitals and camps have no single record of who donated and when.

Our solution: BDMS

  • A public directory searchable by blood group and city.
  • Donors register once and switch availability on or off themselves.
  • Admins publish emergency blood requests and track them to completion.
  • Every donation and every availability change is recorded for audit.
  • Donor contact details stay private.

4. Feature Walkthrough

PUBLIC

Login

Secure sign-in for donors and administrators. Passwords are stored as BCrypt hashes, never as plain text.

Wrong passwords, unknown emails and deactivated accounts all get the same message, so attackers learn nothing.

PUBLIC

Donor registration

Anyone can join as a donor in one form: name, email, password, blood group, date of birth, phone, city and area.

The form is validated: valid email, password of 8 to 72 characters, valid phone number, date of birth in the past, and a unique email.

PUBLIC

Find donors

The home page lists every available donor with blood group, city and area.

Phone numbers and emails are hidden from the public list to prevent scraping and spam.

PUBLIC

Search by blood group and city

Filters can be combined, for example O+ in Mumbai. City matching ignores upper and lower case.

Only donors who are marked available appear, so the result is always someone who can help now.

PUBLIC

Blood groups

Reference page for all 8 supported blood groups, with a shortcut to search donors of each group.

PUBLIC

Open blood requests

Everyone can see requests that still need blood (Pending and In progress), with urgency: Normal, Urgent or Critical.

Requests can be filtered by blood group, city and status. Fulfilled and cancelled requests are not listed.

ADMIN

Administrator console

Live statistics: total donors, available donors, blood requests and total donations.

Shortcuts to the main tasks, and a table of the most recent blood requests.

ADMIN

Create a blood request

Admins enter patient, hospital, city, contact number, blood group, units and urgency.

Rules enforced: units must be 1 or more, contact number must be valid, and the required-by date cannot be in the past.

ADMIN

Request details

Full record of one request: patient, hospital, urgency, status, dates and the administrator who recorded it.

Admins get an Update Request Status button while the request is still open.

ADMIN

Request status workflow

Allowed flow: Pending → In progress → Fulfilled or Cancelled.

Going backwards, repeating a status, or changing a fulfilled or cancelled request is blocked with a clear message.

ADMIN

Record a donation

Admins log a completed donation with donor, date, location and remarks.

Rules: the date cannot be in the future or earlier than the donor's previous donation, and the donor must exist. The donor's last donation date updates automatically.

ADMIN

All donations

A complete register of every donation recorded in the system, with the donor, blood group, place and the admin who recorded it.

ADMIN

Donation history of one donor

Admins can open the history of any single donor, newest donation first.

ADMIN

Availability audit trail

Every time a donor switches between available and unavailable, a record is kept: previous status, new status, time and who changed it.

This history cannot be edited from the application.

ADMIN

User account control

Admins can activate or deactivate any account. A deactivated donor can no longer log in.

An administrator cannot deactivate their own account, so the system can never lock itself out.

DONOR

Donor dashboard

After login, a donor sees their blood group, city, availability and last donation date at a glance.

DONOR

Profile & availability switch

Donors see their full profile and can switch Available / Unavailable themselves, for example after a recent illness or travel.

Setting the same status twice is rejected, and a donor can only change their own availability.

DONOR

My availability history

Donors can review their own status changes and see who made each change.

DONOR

My donation history

A personal record of all verified donations, each with a View Certificate link.

DONOR

Donation certificate

Details of one donation: date, place, blood group and who verified it.

A donor can only open their own certificates. Trying another donor's link redirects back to their own history.

5. Security & Data Integrity

Passwords

  • BCrypt hashing with salt
  • No plain-text passwords stored
  • Max length enforced

Sessions

  • New session id on login (fixation defense)
  • Session id only in cookie, never in the URL
  • Logout ends the session

Access control

  • Admin pages blocked for donors and visitors
  • Donor pages blocked for visitors
  • Donors see only their own data

Privacy

  • Phone and email hidden from the public list
  • Same error for wrong email or password

Database integrity

  • Primary and foreign keys
  • CHECK constraints on roles, statuses, blood groups, urgency
  • Unique email

Validation

  • Bean validation on every form
  • Business rules in the service layer
  • Friendly messages instead of crashes

Examples of rules proven by the tests

  • Wrong password, unknown email and empty form are all rejected.
  • Duplicate email, short password, bad phone and future birth date block registration.
  • Visitors and donors are redirected away from every admin page.
  • A request cannot move backwards or change after it is fulfilled.
  • A donation cannot be future-dated, older than the previous donation, or for an unknown donor.
  • An admin cannot deactivate their own account; a deactivated donor cannot log in.
  • A donor cannot open another donor's donation certificate.

7. Deployment & Future Scope

How it is deployed

  • GitHub: source code, one branch (main)
  • Neon: managed PostgreSQL database
  • Render: builds the Dockerfile and runs the Java application; database settings come from environment variables
  • Vercel: public web address that forwards every request to the Java application
  • A built-in keep-alive ping every 10 minutes stops the free Render service from going to sleep

Future scope

  • SMS and email alerts to matching donors when a critical request is created
  • Eligibility rule: block donors who donated in the last 90 days
  • Hospital and blood-bank logins
  • Map-based donor search
  • Admin reports and CSV export
  • Mobile app
